Workflow · Chapter I

We treat every upload
as a version to be kept,
not a file to be replaced.

Quire is a collaborative media pipeline. A project holds its assets; every asset holds its versions; every version carries its own processing state, its review, and the record of who approved it and when. Nothing is overwritten in place.

Uploads are processed into derivatives people can actually look at: thumbnails, proxies, previews sized for the room they will be seen in. Reviewers annotate the frame, not a filename. Approvals are explicit, dated, and undone only by another decision.

There is no house style here, only a house temperament: keep everything, show the state plainly, and make the history readable by the people who were not in the room.

    Lifecycle · Chapter IV

    Four states, no shortcuts.

    State 01

    Upload

    A file arrives and becomes a version — numbered, hashed, attached to its asset. The source is kept exactly as it came, alongside everything made from it.

    State 02

    Process

    The pipeline makes derivatives from the source: thumbnails, proxies, previews. The state is visible while it runs, and a failure is a state with a reason, not a mystery.

    State 03

    Review

    Reviewers see the version, not the folder. Notes are pinned to the frame they were written on, and nothing moves forward until someone says so.

    State 04

    Approve & deliver

    One decision, dated and attributed. The approved version ships with its history — every earlier version, every note, every state it passed through.
